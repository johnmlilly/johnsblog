There's something simple about writing. It's just you and the words you put on the page. You're not worried about how things look or how they're designed. It's all about what's written.

[My path to software development](https://johnlilly.dev/blog/how-i-became-a-web-developer/) started not only with a love for building and tinkering with things, but also with writing. I've loved it for a long time because it lets you say exactly what you mean. Visuals can leave a lot open to interpretation, but with words you can be direct with no fluff.

As my career moved from writing to coding, that idea stuck with me. Coding is just you and your editor (with some help from AI these days). What you put down is up to you.

## The Change

If you've been to my site before, you probably noticed things look a little different. The whole site now looks like a terminal. Part of that was just for fun, but mostly it reflects how I've been working lately.

I've been spending a lot more time in the terminal, and I've come to love how simple it is. There's no layout to fight with and no buttons to hunt for. You type what you want to do, hit enter, and it happens.

That's especially true with AI tools like [Claude Code](https://claude.com/product/claude-code) and [Cursor](https://cursor.com). Instead of clicking through menus or jumping between windows, I describe what I need in plain words and stay focused on the actual work. Most of this redesign happened that way. I'd type something like "make the nav links smaller" or "use the same icons as the services section," look at the result, and keep going.

That's what reminds me of writing. Some of my best writing happens in a plain document with nothing else on the screen. A terminal feels the same way. You focus on what you're trying to do, not on how it looks.

Which is a funny thing for a web developer to say, since how things look is a big part of my job. Don't get me wrong, design matters a lot, maybe more now than ever in this AI era. But even when working on frontend UI, I want my focus on my work with no distractions.

## New Site, Same Me

I wanted my site to still showcase my career, my goals, where I've been and what I've learned along the way. But I also wanted it to feel more uniquely me. A few details I had the most fun with:

- The different home page sections styled as terminal inputs and outputs, including the latest GitHub activity pulled directly from my actual profile
- Under the hood, moving the site from 11ty to Astro. Better support and integration with [Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/) (my go-to platform for hosting and deployment these days)
- [Projects](https://johnlilly.dev/projects/) and [posts](https://johnlilly.dev/blog/) show up as neofetch-style cards with pixel-art logos, generated automatically from their tags. The [social preview image](/projects/social-previews/) for every page is built from those same cards

That last one is my favorite. It all runs on its own, so I don't have to hunt for an image every time I publish a post.

If you haven't spent much time in a terminal, give it a try. Open one up, poke around, and see how it feels to just type what you need. You might like the quiet.
