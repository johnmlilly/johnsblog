If you've been here before, you probably noticed things look a little different. The whole site now looks like a terminal: monospace text, a blinking cursor, blog posts listed like a log file, and a little status bar at the bottom of the screen.

Part of it was just for fun. But mostly it reflects how I've been working lately.

I've been spending a lot more time in the terminal, and I've come to love how simple it is. There's no layout to fight with and no buttons to hunt for. You type what you want to do, hit enter, and it happens. Everything else gets out of the way.

That's especially true with AI tools like Claude Code and Cursor. Instead of clicking through menus or jumping between windows, I describe what I need in plain words and stay focused on the actual work. Most of this redesign happened that way. I'd type something like "make the nav links smaller" or "use the same icons as the services section," look at the result, and keep going.

It reminds me a lot of writing. I've always loved to write, and some of my best writing happens in a plain document with nothing else on the screen. I'm not thinking about fonts or formatting. It's just me and the words. A terminal feels the same way to me. You focus on what you're trying to do, not on how it looks.

Which is a funny thing for a web developer to say, since how things look is a big part of my job. When I build sites for clients, design matters a lot. But when I'm the one doing the work, I want my tools to fade into the background so I can think.

So I wanted my site to feel like that too. A few details I had the most fun with:

- The services section is an `ls -la` listing.
- Projects and posts show up as neofetch-style cards with pixel-art logos, generated automatically from their tags.
- The social preview image for every page is built from those same cards, so I never have to make one by hand again.

That last one is my favorite. Set it up once, and then just write.

If you haven't spent much time in a terminal, give it a try. Open one up, poke around, and see how it feels to just type what you need. You might like the quiet.
